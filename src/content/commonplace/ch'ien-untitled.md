UNTITLED

I couldn’t want another life. Tending<br>
fields and mulberries—it’s my true

calling. I’ve never failed it, and still,<br>
against hunger and cold, there’s only

hull and chaff. I never wanted more<br>
than a full stomach. All I’ve asked is

a little rice, heavy clothes for winter<br>
and open-weaves for summer heat.

But I haven’t even managed that. O,<br>
all this grief cuts deep. And character

is fate. If you’re simpleminded, life’s<br>
ways elude you. It’s the inner pattern:

no one’s likely to change it. But then,<br>
I delight in even a single cup of wine.

-- T'ao Ch'ien, translated by David Hinton, in _Classical Chinese Poetry_

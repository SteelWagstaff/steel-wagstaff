> I had one baby behind the next. I&rsquo;m the mother of seven. I had to spend quite a lot of time with my family, because I never believed in leaving my children with anybody. I taught my kids to stand up and be counted. And I learned them to don&rsquo;t be afraid of nobody. And because a man&rsquo;s skin was white, he wasn&rsquo;t a bit better than they was. But then I told them they weren&rsquo;t any better than anybody else. You could be a dog if you wanted to or you could be a man if you wanted to. I said, &lsquo;Nobody give respect to you&ndash;you&rsquo;re going to have to take it. But you don&rsquo;t have to go 'round her &ldquo;Yessir, No sir, Mister, Boss&rdquo; and all this kind of stuff. That wasn&rsquo;t respect. If he call you Jim, you call him Mike. And if he&rsquo;s nice to you, be nice to him. Always smile first at a person to see whether he goin&rsquo; smile back. But he don&rsquo;t smile back, don&rsquo;t you keep on smilin&rsquo;,&rsquo; I said, 'because directly he think you Aunt Jane. ANd wherever you go, don&rsquo;t you go look for no seat amongst black people. You go anyplace that black and white is sittin&rsquo; there, wherever a seat is vacant, sit down. Because as long as you is a citizen of the United States&rsquo;&ndash;I said not even South Carolina but the United States&ndash;'then nobody is entitled to any more than you.&rsquo;<br/> And I taught them that from babies on the breast. And all my children today&ndash;and I&rsquo;m not braggin&rsquo;&ndash;they&rsquo;re some of the nicestest kids you ever met. You talk with 'em, you&rsquo;ll want to talk with 'em again. But you&rsquo;ll find out they&rsquo;re not afraid of you a bit.

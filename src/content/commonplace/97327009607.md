> <p>The sum of human wisdom is not contained in any one language, and no single language is CAPABLE of expressing all forms and degrees of human comprehension. &hellip;</p> <p>AT ABOUT THIS POINT the weak-hearted reader usually sits down in the road, removes his shoes and weeps that he &lsquo;is a bad linguist&rsquo; or that he or she can&rsquo;t possibly learn all those languages.</p> <p>One has to divide the readers who want to be experts from those who do not, and divide, as it were, those who want to see the world from those who merely want to know WHAT PART OF IT THEY LIVE IN.</p> <p>When it comes to the question of poetry, a great many people don&rsquo;t even want to know that their own country does not occupy ALL the available surface of the planet. The idea seems in some way to insult them.</p>

— Ezra Pound, _<a href="https::::://monoskop.org/images/a/a4/Pound_Ezra_ABC_of_Reading.pdf" target="_blank">ABC of Reading</a>. _A broad-minded thought, to be sure, though the &lsquo;merely&rsquo; wounds me. It seems a life&rsquo;s work, and more, to come to know even the small part of the world one lives in. See Bunting&rsquo;s &ldquo;Briggflatts,&rdquo; for example.

> <p>Yes, you’ll need to stay alive<br/> to the possibilities<br/> of disappointing us, who believe<br/> you’d change everything</p> <p>&amp; yesterday. To dodge bullets,<br/> those ballots of the disaffected<br/> &amp; the entrenched,<br/> their undisclosed locations</p> <p>in our collective mind. To stare<br/> whole buildings back<br/> from rubble of foreclosure<br/> &amp; condemnation, stand watch</p> <p>over bridges to nowhere <br/> we’ve never known, &amp; always<br/> someone’s somewhere. To end stop<br/> /loss &amp; unlawful</p> <p>combatants, extraordinary<br/> renditions &amp; waterboarding,<br/> the cool abstractions that make<br/> torture into Pet Sounds.</p> <p>You’ll need to pierce the wall<br/> of sound that power makes,<br/> or tent yourself in your living<br/> room &amp; slowly go mad. Stay long</p> <p>enough that we grow<br/> used to you, scion of the globe,<br/> become mundane as a dollar, <br/> flawed, iconic, yet alive. </p> <p>So when you’re called to kill <br/> in our name, like a lover<br/> who’s slept with another,<br/> we’ll never let you live</p> <p>it down, though we will<br/> never leave. Nor forgive.</p>

— &ldquo;Letter to Barack Obama (Solstice, 2008)&rdquo; by Philip Metres (pmetres@jcu.edu)

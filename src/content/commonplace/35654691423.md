To Petrarch love was mainly an excuse for displaying his skill as a versifier and his knowledge of classical mythology. He hardly ever pays any real attention to Laura: he focuses the reader’s attention on his own cleverness, and that cleverness is far too often trivial, quite often a matter of puns. Petrarch’s verse is from the first aimed directly at the coronation with laurel which he did indeed achieve in Rome. He had no humility. He loved neither the countess de Sade (Laura) nor God. Too many of his lines are mere perfunctory decoration.                    — Basil Bunting, administering pungent, moral literary criticism of real worth to many, many, many of the poets now writing in America. From his chapter on Thomas Wyat[t] in Basil Bunting on Poetry

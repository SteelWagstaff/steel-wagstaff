> <p>The body surges. Out of necessity, or for the love of movement.</p> <p>Lifestyles and industries pulse around it, groping for what to make of the way it throws itself at objects of round perfection.</p> <p>The way it builds its substance out of layers of sensory impact.</p> <p>The way the body is submerged in a flow and both buoyed and carried away. It strains against recalcitrant or alien forces, or it drifts downstream, eyes trained on the watery clouds overhead.</p> <p>Agency lodged in the body is literal, immanent, and experimental. It no sooner starts out than it gets sidetracked or hits a wall and then holes up, bulks up, wraps around itself. It might pull together or pull a veil around itself, build a nest of worn clothing redolent with smells of sweat, or cheap perfume, or smoky wood fires burrowed into wool. If it gets sluggish, it might call for sweet and heavy things to match its inner weight, or for salt and caffeine to jolt it to attention. </p> <p>The body knows itself as states of vitality, immersion, isolation, exhaustion, and renewal.</p> <p>It can be alert to the smell of something sweet or rancid in the air or to a movement too quick, a gesture that’s a little off. </p> <p>It can be ponderous, too, gazing on its own form with a zenlike emptiness. As a new lover, it dotes on revealed scars and zones in on freckles and moles and earlobes. As one of the anxious aging, it’s drawn to the sight of new jowls and mutant hairs and mottled skin in the bathroom mirror.</p> <p>The body is both the persistent site of self-recognition and the thing that always betrays us. It dreams of redemption but it knows better than that too.</p> <p>It loves and dreads the encounters that make it. It latches onto a borrowed intimacy or a plan of some sort. </p> <p>Layers of invented life form around the body’s dreamy surges like tendons or fat.</p>

— Kathleen Stewart, &ldquo;The Body Surges&rdquo; from <em>Ordinary Affects </em>

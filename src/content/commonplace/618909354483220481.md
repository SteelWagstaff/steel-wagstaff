> Poems from Han Shan 87Greedy men love to store wealthlike owls love their babies,but small owls eat their mother when grown,and the self is hurt by too much wealth.Give away your wealth and you are blessed;gathering it makes disasters rise.With no wealth and no disasters,you can glide on wings in blue clouds.100The life and death metaphorcan be found in ice and water.Water solidifies into ice;ice dissolves into water.The dead are going to be born;once born they’ll be dead again.Ice and water do not hurt each other.Life and death are perfect complements.128An elegant, poised, and handsome young man,well versed in canons and histories,everyone calls him a teacher,or addresses him as a scholar,but he fails to obtain an official position,and he does not know how to farm.He wears only a shabby gown in winter,totally ruined by books.158There’s a tree that existed before the woods,in age twice as old.Its roots suffered as the valley changed,its leaves deformed by wind and frost.People all laugh at its withered aspect,caring nothing about the core’s beauty.When the bark is all stripped off,only essence remains.210Talking about food won’t fill your stomach.Talk about clothing won’t keep out cold.To be full, eat rice.To stay warm, eat clothes.Those who don’t understandcomplain it’s hard to get help from Buddha.Look inside your heart. That’s where Buddha is.Don’t look for him outside.266A word to meat eaters:you eat without pause,but this life is planted in the pastand the future’s shaped by today.If you live for great flavorsfearless of the next life’s billyou’re like a rat in a rice steamer,so full you can’t get out.– Han Shan, translated from the Chinese by Tony Barnstone and Chou Ping

> I can’t tell you what is good or bad in this book of verse. The only one who can decide that is you yourself in answer to the question what do you really want to say. I can only tell you what I like and why I like it. But you must not let yourself be misled by that—and you won’t be.<br/> As for the technique of saying what you want to say, you know as much about it as I do. You have sufficient interest in the meaning and sound of words and sentences and you know that the sound and rhythm is part of the meaning: that is why we write in verse—when we do.

— Charles Reznikoff, letter to George Oppen [Feburary 7, 1962]. &ldquo;This book of verse&rdquo; was Oppen&rsquo;s _The Materials_, his first to appear in almost 30 years. This is the only letter from Reznikoff to Oppen to appear in Reznikoff&rsquo;s <a href="http://www.blacksparrowbooks.com/isbn.asp?isbn=1574230352">selected letters</a>.

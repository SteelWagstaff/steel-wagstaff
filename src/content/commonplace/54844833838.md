> Infinite is a meaningless word: except &ndash; it states/ That our mind is capable of performing/ an endless process of addition.

— Louis Zukofsky, &quot;A-8&quot;, quoting Friederich Engels: &ldquo;An <em>unbodily substance</em> is the same absurdity as an <em>unbodily body</em>. <em>Body, being</em>, <em>substance</em>, are but different terms for the same <em>reality</em>. It is impossible to separate thought from matter <em>that</em> thinks. This matter is the substratum of all changes going on in the world. The word <em>infinite</em> is <em>meaningless</em>, unless it states that our mind is capable of performing an endless process of addition. Only material things being perceptible, knowable to us, we cannot know <em>anything</em> about the existence of God. My own existence alone is certain. Every human passion is a mechanical movement which has a beginning and an end. The objects of impulse are what we call good. Man is subject to the same laws as nature. Power and freedom are identical.&rdquo;

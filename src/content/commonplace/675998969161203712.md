Once again the world seems to be less about objects than about interactive relationships. … Just as the idea of a continuous space that contains things disappears, so the idea of an elementary and primal ‘time’ flowing regardless of things also vanishes. … This doesn’t mean that everything is stationary and unchanging … it means that change is ubiquitous – but elementary processes cannot be ordered in a common succession of ‘instants’. At the minute scale of the grains of space, the dance of nature does not take place to the rhythm of the baton of a single orchestral conductor, at a single tempo: each process dances independently of its neighbors, to its own rhythm. The passage of time is internal to the world, is born in the world itself in the relationship between quantum events that comprise the world and are themselves the source of time.

The world described by the theory is thus further distanced from the one with which we are familiar. There is no longer space which ‘contains’ the world, and there is no longer time ‘in which’ events occur. There are only elementary processes wherein quanta of space and matter continually interact with each other. The illusion of space and time which continues around us is a blurred vision of this swarming of elementary processes.

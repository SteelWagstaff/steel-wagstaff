> But when sincerity in writing is present the insincere may be cut out at will and information, not ignorance, remains. Sincerity among authors differs with the range of their sensations and apperceptions, but what is negative to sincerity remains negative to all who are sincere. So much that is vicious, as writing, is omitted from all of them, and of these there are probably no more than can be counted on the fingers of both hands in a generation. Reznikoff is included among these. One speaks of sincerity as of that ability necessary for existence if one is a writer. Naturally, the bubble does not know what a more actual body brush ing past it may do to it, and in a minute it is not even a bubble.

— Louis Zukofsky, <a href="https://www.jstor.org.ezproxy.library.wisc.edu/stable/pdfplus/20577923.pdf?acceptTC=true&amp;">&ldquo;Sincerity and Objectification&rdquo;</a>

I can’t remember how I heard of a new magazine, Golden Goose, but it may have been that I found a copy on the table for new arrivals at Gordon Cairnie’s Grolier Book Shop. At any rate, it impressed me. A magazine, very modest in its format, which contained poems that interested me. I had become increasingly dissatisfied with the pronouncements of official house organs such as Kenyon and Partisan Review. The erudite commentaries appended to poems that were increasingly obscure merely clouded the issue further. Certainly none of this was moving in a direction that was attractive to me. One of the pundits had actually said that “poetry is a superior parlor game.” That angered me. Here on the other hand was a magazine that made no claims, merely published poems.                    — Theodore Enslin, on discovering Frederick Eckman, Richard Wirtz Emerson, and Robert Beum’s little magazine Golden Goose.

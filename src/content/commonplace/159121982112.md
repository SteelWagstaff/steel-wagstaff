In fact, we arrived in New York on the strength of what poetry we knew. We had left San Francisco thinking, really I think, of the Middle West, though I had been born in New York and we wanted to return. The poetry we knew at that time and had responded to, although we had noted Williams and we had noted Pound, but I think what brought us to hitchhiking across the country was primarily the Mid-West poets. People like Sherwood Anderson, even people like Sandburg–the whole sense of all we didn’t know and that the poets didn’t seem to know, the standard poets.                    — George Oppen, talking with Charles Amirkhanian on a 1976 KPFA broadcast, published in Speaking with George Oppen, p. 120.

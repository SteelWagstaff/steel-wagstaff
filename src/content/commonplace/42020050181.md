> What is it about nearness to the singular object that moves a person so? Is it perhaps the sense that the past is present, that some fragment of a time we never experienced has survived, as if to speak directly to us in the present? Or is it that we are not only close to &ldquo;the past&rdquo; in a general sense, but are witness to the evidence of the moment of creation &ndash; the single page on which a poem came into being and the moment itself, as if the past did not exist at all, was simply a part of the present we hadn&rsquo;t yet encountered?

— <a href="http://pearlblossomhighway.blogspot.com/2013/01/aimless-reading-zs-part-15-louis.html">Michael Kelleher</a>

> Basil Bunting to George Oppen I believe this is the only surviving letter written by Basil Bunting to George Oppen, quoted in its entirety from the Oppen papers in the Mandeville Special Collections at UCSD: Basil Bunting Shadingfield Wylam Northumberland [stationary header] 16 January 1973 Dear Mr Oppen Thankyou for the book. I do not think that I am qualified to say anything about it. It is, to begin with, American and I am not; and it seems, secondly, to be written by someone who knows what’s going on amongst poets, and I dont. You have, you Americans, tasks - creating a language, inventing a history - which we escape in England. That leaves our poets a freedom they dont know what to do with, so that they seem and usually are aimless. On the other hand it leaves some of yours unaware that skill and patience are wanted. I’ve wondered lately whether intelligence and reading are not handicaps, very poor substitutes for a share eye or an attentive ear. All my generation, which isnt so much older than yours, were too bookish, except WCW. Yet I’m disgusted by the crowd of writers who have read only Time or some less durable antagonist of Time. Perhaps a poet’s training should be in biology or physics. Too many of us live in echo chambers. Now I see I’ve written too much for civility and perhaps too little for the something more than civility I wanted to show. I’d better end Yours faithfully"

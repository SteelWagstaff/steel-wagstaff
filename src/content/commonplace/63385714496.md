  <p><strong>“Owed to UTerUS”</strong> (by Wendy Vardaman)</p>
  <p>This visual poem is part of “T<a href=\"https::::://www.pinterest.com/profgates/exquisite-uterus-preview-board/\">he Exquisite Uterus Project</a>,” curated by Alison Gates &amp; Helen Klebesadel, and on&nbsp;exhibit at the <a hrehttps:s:s:s:s://www.aux.uwm.edu/union/art_gallery/\">UW-Milwaukee Union Art Gallery</a> through October 11. As a poet, I work with words more than visual material, except when doing graphic design and book covers. I’ve been admiring visual poetry for a long time and enjoyed the chance to bring visual and poetic thinking together a little more in “Owed to UTerUS,” which displays the lines of a visual poem I wrote in expanding circles from the printed uterus, with poetic “Idioms from the RePublic of UTerUS” scribbled around the other poem. The idioms take expressions about other body parts and replace those words with “uterus,” which has no idiomatic expressions. The Xpansive UTerUS rejects invisibility: is language and body, is inside and outside, is connection, is US. </p>
  <p>The exhibit, open to all, brings together over 200 fiber &amp; mixed-media artworks from across the country and gives voice to issues around reproductive rights, healthcare, women’s bodies and control.&nbsp;</p>

**Allen Ginsberg:**
With any of you, does the occasion of writing [...] create or loosen or or or present to your field of attention um the elements of the poem ever or do you find that you are uh sitting in the middle of a poem, that is an arrangement of objects around you related to you that you write down, in other words, do you ever start writing, you know does the fact that you are a writer and poet uh remind you that everything has a relationship, or are you sitting there on uh a waiting bench and see a fly and see the relationships and so write it down?   

**George Oppen:**

**Ginsberg:**
Do you have a habit of mind of looking for relationships, I should say?  

**Carl Rakosi:**
No. No.  

**Charles Reznikoff:**
I would say no too.  

**Rakosi:**
Actually most of the time I don't think of myself as a poet uh, you know. You know it, you know it has to, it has come up on me.  

**Ginsberg:**
Most of the time you're not thinking about observing objects for rearrangement in language to put down.  

**Oppen:**
I don't.  

**Rakosi:**
I don't go around looking for subjects for poetry. Matter of fact I try to avoid it.  

**Reznikoff:**
No, what I would say of myself, what are you talking ...  

**Oppen:**
Everytime I sit down, anytime I sit down at a desk, around me gathers the sense of something like a sphere and some object will seem to me a way to express this [and?] in the course of the poem the fly may disappear and something entirely different will appear. My sense is [indeed?] of being alone in those times of being alone with a piece of paper and the piece of paper in a way becomes the field on which all this, well, I'm just speaking of my visual focus ...

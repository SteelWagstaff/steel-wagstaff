> When our French teacher, as background to Racine, gave us a course on tragic drama, reaching back to the Greeks, and actually quoted from Aristotle&rsquo;s Poetics, my heart stood still. He explained all the Greek terms like peripeteia and anagnorisis to us. So when I saw Puccini&rsquo;s La Bohème (my very first opera) at the local theater, I noted how many of these terms applied to the Puccini and, back at school, told him of this. “Yes,” he said, “but rather cheaply embodied.” My feelings were a little dashed. I still think he was somewhat hard on Puccini, but the salutary challenge introduced me to the notion of critical standards, and the realization that terminology in the abstract couldn&rsquo;t really help you with the question of quality. A good early warning about the futility of theory.

— Charles Tomlinson, in <a href="https::://www.theparisreview.org/interviews/1033/the-art-of-poetry-no-78-charles-tomlinson">an interview</a> published in the _Paris Review_. Read it. It&rsquo;s amazing.

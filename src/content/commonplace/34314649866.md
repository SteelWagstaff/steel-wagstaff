> And once more, I read the Black Hawk War and Old Chief Black Hawk, who lost his women and children in the swamps where I live so the white army wouldn’t find ‘em. After I read about the Black Hawk War I don’t think much of white people. That war started (Stillman’s Defeat) with Black Hawk and his men crossing the Mississippi River over into Ill. with the white flag of truce, and our soldiers didn’t stop for that, just shot ‘em down. Again, later, then the Indian band was reduced to almost nothing, starved, without clothes, getting themselves back over the Mississippi as best they could, in spite of their white flag they were mowed down. Then Black Hawk was captured and taken on a trip over the east, shown to the President, and he dictated his life to a Frenchman and that’s one of my great books. He said, “Rock River was a beautiful country. I liked my town, my cornfields and the home of my people. I fought for them.” His town was Rock Island, Ill. or near there. &hellip; I will end by quoting myself - I omitted the first letter of a word and it got handed in and the typists called my attention to it: (I&rsquo;m writing about Alexander Hamilton&rsquo;s pioneer son) &ldquo;Hamilton reconnoitered and found Black Hawk not where White Crow had led them to believe, but on Bark River on a pot very advantageous to himself. &hellip;&rdquo; Suzz. He should have emptied it on the heads of the whites.

— Letter from Lorine Niedecker to Louis Zukofsky, May 18, 1941 [she was working in Madison for the WPA as a writer and researcher in the Federal Writers&rsquo; Project]

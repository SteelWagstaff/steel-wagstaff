From the Oppen papers held by the Mandeville Special Collections Library, UCSD

from George Oppen’s initial letter [June 24, 1962]: I thought very highly of your poems: it is not a matter of praising the poems only for what is not stuck into them but among the things I would like to say of them is that singleness of purpose, which is honesty of purpose, the absence of hedging a bet is still a virtue and a still a rare one. I was very much moved.

from William Bronk’s response [June 25, 1962]: I think of poetry as a statement about our experience which is said to someone, as the start of a conversation. It nobody answers I have that flat feeling that what I have said must be trivial or dull or so much out of the way that no one else wants to associate himself with it. I am embarrassed and and disappointed.

But now I am pleased that I was somehow able to get through to you and evidently said something that seemed important and common enough that you were willing to take the trouble to respond to it.

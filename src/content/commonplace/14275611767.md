Using trial and error, we have shaped our world from caves to skyscrapers, from horses to jet planes, from spears to hydrogen bombs. Cultural or religious rituals (e.g., rain dance) and superstition may have developed as a means to create a feeling of control where, ultimately, there is none. Clearly, behaving flexibly in order to control our environment is at the heart of human nature and probably affects more aspects of our daily lives than any other brain function. So essential is such functioning that even very simple brains possess it. Even Drosophila [genus of fly more commonly referred to as fruit flies] prefers a situation in which it controls its environment over one where it does not.                    — Bjorn Brembs, “The Importance of Being Active”

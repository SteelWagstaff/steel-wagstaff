content: "Early Women’s Basketball at UW-Madison uwmadarchives: With the beginning of both Women’s History Month and March Madness, it seemed only fitting to say a little about early women’s basketball on campus. Women’s basketball team, 1897The Wisconsin State Journal of May 3, 1897 reported: “Next Friday afternoon [May 7] a contest which will be both novel and interesting will be held in the ladies gymnasium… A match game of basket ball will be played by two teams of ladies, the event closing a most successful year’s work in the ladies gymnasium. It will be an invitation affair, and the invitations will be restricted to ladies, married men and their wives, and men who have reached ’’years of discretion:” no interpretation is given of the latter condition, however. There is room for about 240 spectators.” Thus began women’s basketball at UW-Madison.For a couple of years the women played teams from some local high schools and the Milwaukee Normal School. By 1900, however, women’s basketball had become only inter-class games, and it stayed that way for over 60 years.Photo from 1903 Badger YearbookWomen’s sports were under the jurisdiction of the Women’s Athletic Association after its founding around 1903, and they basically remained under the WAA or WRA (Women’s Recreation Association) until Kit Saunders was hired to oversee women’s sports in 1967.Page from WAA scrapbook of Elsie Howell, class of 1918.As you can see in the photo below from 1916, women’s games did attract spectators. This game was in Lathrop Hall, then the women’s gymnasium. We do not have as many photos of women playing basketball between the 1930s and 1970s, but women continued to play the sport intramurally until the advent of women’s varsity basketball in 1974/75. Women students playing basketball, c. 1935.David Null for the University Archives. ********** For more information about UW campus history, contact uwarchiv@library.wisc.edu or visit library.wisc.edu/archives. On, Wisconsin!"

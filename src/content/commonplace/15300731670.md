In painting, a still life is a genre that captures the livens of inanimate things (fruit, flowers, bowls) by suspending their sensory beauty in an intimate scene charged with the textures of paint and desire. … A still life is a static state filled with vibratory motion, or resonance. A quivering in the stability of a category or a trajectory, it gives the ordinary the charge of an unfolding.                    — Kathleen Stewart, Ordinary Affects

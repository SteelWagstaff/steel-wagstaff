> <p>In my work, I’ve always attempted to make sense at higher and higher speeds. To combine, as Mao said, being aesthetically powerful but politically revolutionary. The most dangerous thing in the world is a really accomplished artist who has a backward political line, because they can convince you of any stupid thing! So my question has always been — as I got conscious — how can you say things that you find politically progressive while utilizing the most powerful aesthetic that you can understand? For me, that aesthetic is rooted in talking about reality, not abstractions.</p> <p>Young poets often start by wanting to talk about themselves: they hurt, they feel, they lost their baby. But to start talking about the world with the same kind of intensity? That’s what I strive to do.</p>

— <a href="https::://flavorwire.com/12827/exclusive-amiri-baraka-and-henry-grimes-idea-of-rhythm">Amiri Baraka</a>

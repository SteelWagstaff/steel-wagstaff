> <p>I SUGGEST<br/> 1. Compose aloud; poetry is a sound.<br/> 2. Vary rhythm enough to stir the emotion you want but not so as to lose impetus.<br/> 3. Use spoken words and syntax.<br/> 4. Fear adjectives; they bleed nouns. Hate the passive.<br/> 5. Jettison ornament gaily but keep shape</p> <p>Put your poem away till you forget it, then:<br/> 6. Cut out every word you dare.<br/> 7. Do it again a week later, and again.</p> <p>Never explain - your reader is as smart as you.</p>

— Basil Bunting&rsquo;s advice for young poets, as given to Ric Caddel&rsquo;s students in the 1970s and later circulated on postcards. His was a lean, sleek, muscular aesthetic, spare and tightly-fitted.

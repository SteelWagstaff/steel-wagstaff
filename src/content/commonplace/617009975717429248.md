Over a muddy winter, Douve, I spread out  
Your face, luminous and low, like a forest.  
Everything dies, I thought, everything vanishes.

I saw you, violent, helplessly laughing,  
At the fall of opulent seasons, Hiding  
With your hair the glare of a livid face.

And I saw you furtive. At the trees’ edge  
Appearing like a fire when the autumn draws  
The whole noise of the storm to the heart of the leaves.

O blackest and most barren! At last I saw you dead,  
Unappeasable lightning bolt strung out on the void,  
Window now put out, and of a dark house.

**– **from *Du mouvement et de l’immobilité de Douve, *translated by Galway Kinnell and Richard Pevear

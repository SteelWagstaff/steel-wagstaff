> Poem by Yves Bonnefoy Over a muddy winter, Douve, I spread outYour face, luminous and low, like a forest.Everything dies, I thought, everything vanishes.I saw you, violent, helplessly laughing,At the fall of opulent seasons, HidingWith your hair the glare of a livid face.And I saw you furtive. At the trees’ edgeAppearing like a fire when the autumn drawsThe whole noise of the storm to the heart of the leaves.O blackest and most barren! At last I saw you dead,Unappeasable lightning bolt strung out on the void,Window now put out, and of a dark house.– from Du mouvement et de l’immobilité de Douve, translated by Galway Kinnell and Richard Pevear

Euphuism and bombast are merely two of the vices of bad poetry, and if they did not mar so much of Shakespeare’s verse there would be no need to pay any attention to them at all. But when Lady Macbeth wants to tell us that the blood on her hands would redden the sea and says instead that it would the multitudinous seas incarnadine we have to be on our guard lest the prestige Shakespeare has earned for his very different characteristics should leave us imagining that this preposterous tirade is poetry.                    — Basil Bunting strikes again.

Poets are the hierophants of an unapprehended inspiration; the mirrors of the gigantic shadows which futurity casts upon the present; the words which express what they understand not; the trumpets which sing to battle, and feel not what they inspire; the influence which is moved not, but moves.                    — Percy Bysshe Shelley, A Defence of Poetry, penultimate sentence, just before “Poets are the unacknowledged legislators of the world.”

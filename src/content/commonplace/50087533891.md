  <p><strong>May 10, 1963: Birmingham Campaign Ends in Victory</strong></p>
  <p>On this day in 1963, the Birmingham Campaign ended with a victory. Martin Luther King, Jr. and Fred Shuttlesworth signed a truce agreement with local officials to outline a ‘limited desegregation plan,’ which promised to:</p>
  <ul>
  <li>Remove “White Only” and “Black Only” signs from restrooms and drinking fountains in downtown Birmingham</li>
  <li>Desegregate lunch counters</li>
  <li>Deploy a “Negro job improvement plan”</li>
  <li>Release jailed demonstrators</li>
  <li>Create a biracial committee to monitor the agreement</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Desegregation, however, would take place slowly over the next few months. Learn more about the <a href=\"http://to.pbs.org/YwMlRl\" title=\"BCC | Birmingham Campaign\">Birmingham Campaign with PBS Black Culture Connection</a>.</p>
  <p><em>Photo: Martin Luther King Jr. gives a press conference regarding an agreement reached on a ‘limited desegregation plan’ outside the Gaston Motel in Birmingham, Alabama (Photo by Ernst Haas/Ernst Haas/Getty Images)</em></p>
  </blockquote>
  <p>Respect. When will we have the will to begin enforcing the Fair Housing legislation that became law in California in 1963 and throughout the nation in 1968? Let’s desegregate our neighborhoods today.</p>

The work of art, Rilke said, says to us always: You must change your life. It demands of us that we too see things as ends, not as means—that we too know them and love them for their own sake. This change is beyond us, perhaps, during the active, greedy, and powerful hours of our lives; but during the contemplative and sympathetic hours of our reading, our listening, our looking, it is surely within our power, if we choose to make it so, if we choose to let one part of our nature to follow its natural desires. So I say to you, for a closing sentence, Read at whim! read at whim!                    — Randall Jarrell, “Poets, Critics, and Readers”

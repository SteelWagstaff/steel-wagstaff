> &hellip; usually it takes the passage of time for the tradition at large to distinguish poetry that is part of the weather from poetry that is part of the landscape. By now, to some of us, Loy&rsquo;s and Bunting&rsquo;s poems are beginning to look like essential features of the geography of twentieth-century poetry in English. In view of the evidence their poems have given of a capacity for persistence even in the face of quite inclement circumstances, it seems to me sensible to inquire into the sources of their durability.

— Jim Powell, &ldquo;Basil Bunting and Mina Loy&rdquo; from an <a href="https:::::://www.jstor.org/stable/i25305470">issue of the _Chicago Review_</a> in 1990 dedicated to &lsquo;neglected poets.&rsquo;

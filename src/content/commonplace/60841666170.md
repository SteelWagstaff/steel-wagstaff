> When I was little, grandmother used to put a hood over her canary&rsquo;s cage to calm it. Who will do that for me? or release me from my mill tasks? Around and around I go like a donkey, further and further from childhood.<br/> You told me to look into people&rsquo;s faces and listen to what they were saying to me &hellip; &ldquo;to get what is really out there&rdquo; &hellip; but, my God, they don&rsquo;t know themselves. They act like tropisms. Or am I too engrossed in my own body sensations &hellip; the smell of grass, stuffed sinuses, all the things I have to see as a painter &hellip; to attend to what is not me?<br/> I&rsquo;ve been stewing in loneliness. Everyone is out of reach. I had a friend once but she moved away. We used to weep together. But I can&rsquo;t do that with others. It would be too revealing, too deranged.

— from Carl Rakosi, &ldquo;The Painter&rdquo; (a series of epistolary fragments from the persona of a female painter/professor) in <em>Ex Cranium, Night.</em>

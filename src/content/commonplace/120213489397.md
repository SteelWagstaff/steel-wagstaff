> from Irving Howe and Eliezer Greenberg’s Introduction to “A Treasury of Yiddish Poetry” Aestheticism is a luxury, and detachment a rarity: not that Yiddish poets do not repeatedly yearn for these, but that their immediate condition of life, as also their final situation as Jews, denies them such possibilities. Is there another Western literature in which the major poets, including those who within the spectrum of Yiddish can be called pure poets, spend their early years, and in some instances the whole of their lives, as shop workers?

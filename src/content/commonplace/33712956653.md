> &hellip; my domestic arts include sewing—occasionally a dress or smock and this reminds me: I have bought some of that so-called cretonne-like material for slip cover—I use a huge, soft, upholstered chair (quite a trick to hold that enormous volume The Letters of Wallace Stevens with one hand and the magnifying glass with another)—not very good to sit down on George Washington whose picture is in the cloth but he cost only 50¢ a yard, about ten times cheaper than anything I preferred.

— Lorine Niedecker to Cid Corman, Nov. 21, 1968.

## Books

If March had a theme for me, I suppose it would have been 'Occupy'? I may be several years late to the movement, but most of what I read this month seemed to have been written by someone involved in the Occupy protests and movements of the past half-dozen years. It's easy to love anarchists when you're only meeting them on the page in the realm of ideas, right? Well, I certainly love _reading_ anarchists. Here’s the best of what I read for pleasure (i.e. not for work or for my dissertation) in March of 2017.

### Nonfiction

-   Finally finished David Graeber's [_Debt: The First 5000 Years_](https://www.mhpbooks.com/books/debt/). One of the best books I've read in recent memory. It took me several months, but was well worth every hour spent on it.
-   After finishing _Debt_ I pretty much immediately read two of Graeber's other books. The first one I tackled was _[The Utopia of Rules: On Technology, Stupidity, and the Secret Joys of Bureaucracy](https://www.mhpbooks.com/books/the-utopia-of-rules/)_. Smart, funny, and insightful, with a strong anarchist slant and a number of creative readings of many of the ubiquitous cultural and political structures that many of us love to hate.
-   I also read Graeber's _[The Democracy Project: A History, A Crisis, A Movement](http://www.randomhousebooks.com/books/220295/)_. Far more pragmatic and anecdotal than the previous two books, with a number of nostalgia-tinged recastings of Occupy's 'success,' but still a worthwhile read. Turned me on to Starhawk, and her [writings on empowerment, group facilitation, and productive 'consensus-driven' meetings](http://starhawk.org/writing/books/the-empowerment-manual/), which was a fortuitous discovery.
-   Cathy O'Neil's terrific [_Weapons of Math Destruction_](https://weaponsofmathdestructionbook.com/). Short, smart, clearly organized and tightly argued, this book about the dangers of poorly designed, opaque algorithms and their impact on nearly every aspect of both public and private life is highly recommended, as is Cathy's [mathbabe](https://mathbabe.org/) blog.
-   I also read Zephyr Teachout's _[Corruption in America](http://www.hup.harvard.edu/catalog.php?isbn=9780674659988&content=reviews)_. It was a fascinating historical study of political corruption, with a cluster of eye-opening episodes I'd never heard about ([Yazoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazoo_land_scandal), anybody?), but it was also definitely written by a lawyer, so _c__aveat lector_.

### Longform Journalism

Against my better judgment I dabbled more than I wanted to in journalism (feels like junk food sometimes, especially when there are so many excellent books I want to read!). Nevertheless, here's what I'd recommend from my shorter non-fiction reading in the month of March.

-   [Miranda Popkey wrote a terrific article for the _New Yorker_ about two of my life heroes: Mary and George Oppen.](http://www.newyorker.com/books/page-turner/when-the-oppens-gave-up-art-to-fight-fascism)
-   I loved and was inspired by [Keeanga-Yamahtta Taylor's generous, capacious article in _Jacobin_](https://www.jacobinmag.com/2017/01/womens-march-dc-trump-protests-inauguration/) on welcoming newcomers to mass social movements and how to avoid the political immaturity that too often characterizes leftist movements.
-   Listened raptly to [Tim Berners-Lee writing in _The Guardian_](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2017/mar/11/tim-berners-lee-web-inventor-save-internet) on what we need to change to save the web.
-   Can't imagine that many of you haven't already seen [Mary Beard's "Women in Power" cover essay](https://www.lrb.co.uk/v39/n06/mary-beard/women-in-power) in the _London Review of Books_, but if you missed it, you probably want to correct your oversight.
-   Was bummed out and outraged by [Liza Mundy's article in _The Atlantic_](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2017/04/why-is-silicon-valley-so-awful-to-women/517788/) about some of the enormous problems tech companies have with treating women decently. Did love the suggestions she outlined that might show some promise in addressing systemic discrimination, many of which were new to me.
-   As a teetotaller in boozy Wisconsin, I found a lot to think about in [Kristi Coulter's rich piece for _Quartz_](https://qz.com/762868/giving-up-alcohol-opened-my-eyes-to-the-infuriating-truth-about-why-women-drink/) about drinking and its relationship to modern womanhood in America.
-   Was definitely piqued by [Stephen Buranyi's description in _the Guardian_](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2017/feb/01/high-tech-war-on-science) of Michèle Nuijten and Chris Hartgerink's brainchild: Statcheck, an algorithm-based approach to detecting and rooting out 'scientific misconduct' in published research.
-   Was troubled and challenged by [Elizabeth Kolbert's article in](http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/02/27/why-facts-dont-change-our-minds) _The New Yorker_ about a spate of recent research on why human beings tend to be worse than we should be at changing our minds when confronted with information which contradicts our present beliefs.
-   Really enjoyed this [Thomas Chatterton Williams profile of John Edgard Wideman](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/01/26/magazine/john-edgar-wideman-against-the-world.html) for the _New York Times_. His book about Emmett Till's father sounds like it'll be outstanding.
-   Respected and admired [Coco Fusco's article on Dana Schutz' controversial painting of Emmett Till's open casket](https://hyperallergic.com/368290/censorship-not-the-painting-must-go-on-dana-schutzs-image-of-emmett-till/) in Hyperallergic.
-   [Mark Hanna wrote a terrific revisionary history piece on pirates](https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2017/winter/feature/lot-what-known-about-pirates-not-true-and-lot-what-true-not-known) for _Humanities_, the magazine of the NEH.
-   [Thomas Nagel reviewed Daniel Dennett's most recent book](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/2017/03/09/is-consciousness-an-illusion-dennett-evolution/) for the _New York Review of Books_. What a treat!
-   Was educated and distressed by a summary of [a study on the effect of new right-wing media outlets on recent political discourse](http://www.cjr.org/analysis/breitbart-media-trump-harvard-study.php) published in the _Columbia Journalism Review_ and written by Yochai Benkler, Robert Faris, Hal Roberts, and Ethan Zuckerman.
-   Loved [George Saunders writing about writing](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2017/mar/04/what-writers-really-do-when-they-write) for _The Guardian_. I'm going to read his _[Lincoln in the Bardo](http://www.georgesaundersbooks.com/lincoln-in-the-bardo/)_ novel soon.
-   Enjoyed [Robbert Dijkgraaf's arguments (even if they were a bit obvious) about the importance of](http://www.chronicle.com/article/We-Need-More-Useless-/239365?key=sp0x03E8c0EpmAD1jKoY1bfV4sUT5Q4XEgqPwngvKWO9K3tB2ItsT7NqOgfc52k3dEZob2E0Tmk2MGVTWEZwZGRZUHdxczhkclJ1U1YyRE0tbFlVMVViMHdPbw) non-utilitiarian science research in _The Chronicle of Higher Education_.
-   Was fascinated by [Simon Parkin's exploration in](http://nautil.us/issue/44/luck/how-designers-engineer-luck-into-video-games) _Nautilus_ of how 'luck' gets engineered into video games
-   Was saddened [to read of the death of Richard Swigg](https://jacket2.org/commentary/memoriam-richard-swigg), a scholar I admired a great deal. He was generous to me, a curious stranger, in ways that most academics would not have been, and I was moved by his kindness and care.
-   Learned a lot about health care, federal policy, and some of the stickier issues around the current 'debate' from [Sarah Kliff and Ezra Klein's Vox article "The Lessons of Obamacare."](http://www.vox.com/policy-and-politics/2017/3/15/14908524/obamacare-lessons-ahca-gop)
-   Despite its terrible click-bait headline, I found [this Jo Marchant article for the _Smithsonian_](http://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/golden-warrior-greek-tomb-exposes-roots-western-civilization-180961441/) about recent archaeological excavations of a Mycenaean grave site at Pylos, near the southwest coast of Greece, really intriguing.
-   Had the bittersweet experience of reading [Torie Rose DeGhett's final edition of 'This Week in War'](http://thepoliticalnotebook.com/day/2017/03/31/). Happy to see her moving on to something better she's chosen for herself, but I'll definitely miss the exhaustive, thorough weekly rundown of global conflict she's been producing over the past few years.

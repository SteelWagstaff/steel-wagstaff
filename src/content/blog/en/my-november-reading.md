## Books

My reading pace slowed a bit in November (the US elections and their sad aftermath have provided me with lots of avenues for distraction and worry), but I still managed to keep up my love affair with books, though I picked a fair amount of duds this month. The poetry and fiction were great, but the nonfiction didn't really light a spark in me. I did read a fair amount of graphic work this month, which was mostly new for me, though it was pretty hit and miss. Outside of the reading I’ve been doing for my dissertation and my work, here’s a list of the books I read for pleasure/self-education in November 2016:

### Poetry

1.  Catherine Jagoe’s [_Bloodroot_](http://www.settlementhouse.us/bloodroot) (a new poetry collection by a friend). The book had several outstanding poems about making one's home in a strange land, and a very impressive, moving section about pregnancy and motherhood.
2.  Robert Lax's _[A Thing That Is](https://www.amazon.com/Thing-That-New-Poems/dp/0879518855)_. Minimalist, contemplative poetry from a tremendously interesting human being.
3.  [_Archaic Greek Poetry: An Anthology_](https://uwpress.wisc.edu/books/0047.htm), edited by Barbara Hughes Foster.
4.  _[The Collected Poems of Kenneth Fearing](http://www.worldcat.org/oclc/284200)_. Fearing was a prominent leftist writer in the 1930s. In the late 1920s he attended the UW-Madison where he lived with the poet Carl Rakosi and was a lover of Margery Latimer's. His poems are angry, cynical, and fairly iconoclastic for their time--it seems me that they anticipate much that would be later developed by American counter-culture writers in the late 1950s and 60s.

### Fiction

1.  Jhumpa Lahiri’s short story collection _[Interpreter of Maladies](http://www.hmhco.com/shop/books/Interpreter-of-Maladies/9780395927205)_ (a birthday gift from my friend Theresa). Highly recommended!

### Non-fiction

1.  Alphonso Lingis' _[The Community of Those Who Have Nothing In Common](http://www.iupress.indiana.edu/product_info.php?products_id=20723)_. A short book made up of dense, often beautiful phenomenologically-inflected reflections on community, illness, embodiment, death, etc..
2.  Catherine Osborne's _[Presocratic Philosophy: A Very Short Introduction](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/presocratic-philosophy-a-very-short-introduction-9780192840943?cc=us&lang=en&)_. Not my favorite book in the series.
3.  Adam Silverstein's [_Islamic History: A Very Short Introduction_](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/islamic-history-a-very-short-introduction-9780199545728?q=islamic%20history&lang=en&cc=us). Covered a lot of ground. Very learned, but I felt that the author's ideological commitments are probably at odds with my own.
4.  Eric Tyson and Ray Brown's _[Home Buying for Dummies](https://www.amazon.com/Home-Buying-Dummies-Eric-Tyson/dp/111919170X/ref=dp_ob_title_bk)_. Not the most engrossing read, but I learned a lot.

### Graphic Works

1.  Alison Bechdel's [_Fun Home: A Family Tragicomic_](https://www.amazon.com/Fun-Home-Tragicomic-Alison-Bechdel/dp/0618871713).
2.  Alison Bechdel's [_Are You My Mother?: A Comic Drama_](https://www.amazon.com/Are-You-My-Mother-Comic/dp/0544002237/ref=pd_sim_14_1?_encoding=UTF8&pd_rd_i=0544002237&pd_rd_r=VSCNTVQ7SD2AS4QQQ64D&pd_rd_w=ijHCb&pd_rd_wg=nHZNz&psc=1&refRID=VSCNTVQ7SD2AS4QQQ64D)
3.  Lynda Barry's [_Picture This_](https://www.drawnandquarterly.com/picture-this).
4.  Brian Vaughan and Niko Henrichon's _[Pride of Baghdad](http://www.vertigocomics.com/graphic-novels/pride-of-baghdad)_. A friend gave it to me and said, I think you'll like this. Sadly, I didn't.
5.  Ted Rall's _[After We Kill You, We Will Welcome You Back As Honored Guests: Unembedded In Afghanistan](https://www.amazon.com/After-Kill-Welcome-Honored-Guests/dp/0809023407)_. I think Rall may be right in many of his foreign policy criticisms, but he seems like an insufferable narcissist in this book. My least favorite of all the books I read this month.

### Currently Reading

I’ve started a few other books that I’m reading more slowly. Each is excellent:

1.  Mary Beard’s _[SPQR: A History of Ancient Rome](http://books.wwnorton.com/books/SPQR/)__._ Slow going, but Beard is an excellent historian and storyteller.
2.  Rebecca Solnit's _Hope in the Dark_. Necessary reading in our current moment. I admire the mind and voice of Rebecca Solnit very, very much.
3.  Jonathan Haidt's _The Righteous Mind: Why Good People are Divided by Politics and Religion_. I'm still curious rather than fully convinced by Haidt's central arguments, though I have been enjoying the self-assessments at [YourMorals](http://www.yourmorals.org/).
4.  Isabel Wilkerson’s _The Warmth of Other Sons._ Not very deep into the book yet, but impressed so far.

## Longform Journalism

I also read a lot of long-form journalism via the Pocket app on my iPad in November. Much of what I read was about politics. I'll spare you all of those pieces. Sans think pieces about recent electoral disasters, my November recommendations include:

1.  "[Being Mortal: Atul Gawande, a moped death, and my mother's Alzheimer's](http://tinfisheditor.blogspot.com/2016/11/being-mortal-atul-gawande-moped-death.html)" reflections on mortality and human fragility by my friend, the poet and professor Susan Schultz.
2.  [This Economist feature on kabaddi](http://www.economist.com/blogs/gametheory/2016/10/forget-cricket) that introduced me to what looks to be a pretty wild sport.
3.  "[How to Be Good](http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/09/05/how-to-be-good)," an old (well, from 2011) Larissa MacFarquhar feature on the moral philosopher Derek Parfit.
4.  "[A lynching in Georgia: the living memorial to America's history of racist violence](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2016/nov/02/a-lynching-in-georgia-the-living-memorial-to-americas-history-of-racist-violence)," in the Guardian, by Peter Baker.
5.  "[Still Running at 119? Not So Fast](http://www.nytimes.com/2016/11/20/sports/119-year-old-runner-world-masters-championships.html?emc=edit_th_20161121&nl=todaysheadlines&nlid=36318574&_r=0&mtrref=undefined),"a fascinating NYT feature about elderly runners by Jeré Longman and Hari Kumar.
6.  Shane Bauer's [piece about going undercover with a border militia](http://www.motherjones.com/politics/2016/10/undercover-border-militia-immigration-bauer) in _Mother Jones_.
7.  William Friedkin's [profile of Father Amorth, the Vatican's chief exorcist](http://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2016/10/father-amorth-the-vatican-exorcist), in Vanity Fair.
8.  John Crowley's [sprawling exploration of American beliefs about aliens](http://bostonreview.net/literature-culture/john-crowley-stranger-things-rise-and-fall-ufos-and-life-moon) in the form of a review two recent books about UFOs in the Boston Review.
